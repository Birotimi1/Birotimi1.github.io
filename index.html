Research

Research interests

My research uses seismic observations to probe Earth's interior structure and understand the physical processes that shape our planet.

Focus Area

Seismic Imaging & Tomography

Building models of Earth's subsurface using seismic wave speeds to map geological structures, from shallow crustal features to deep mantle heterogeneity.

Surface Waves Body Waves Tomography

Focus Area

Ambient Noise Seismology

Extracting useful seismic signals from background noise generated by ocean waves and other sources to image structure where earthquake data is sparse.

Noise Correlations Green's Functions Phase Velocities

Focus Area

Earth Structure & Dynamics

Investigating the structure and evolution of the lithosphere and asthenosphere to understand mantle flow, tectonic deformation, and the role of melt and volatiles.

Mantle Dynamics Lithosphere Anisotropy

About

Background

I'm a member of the Russell Seismology Group at Syracuse University, where I work under the supervision of Prof. Joshua Russell. Our group focuses on using seismic waves to image Earth's crust and upper mantle to understand tectonic processes.

My work sits at the intersection of geophysics, signal processing, and computational science. I enjoy the full cycle of seismological research — from acquiring and processing seismic data to building models that reveal new insights about Earth's interior.

Methods

Seismic tomography, ambient noise, surface-wave analysis, receiver functions

Tools

Python, MATLAB, ObsPy, SAC, GMT, QGIS

Computation

Signal processing, inverse theory, numerical modeling, data visualization

Interests

Mantle dynamics, lithospheric structure, seismic anisotropy, ocean-bottom seismology
